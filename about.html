About Quizzr

Quizzr is a free site of practice questions for AP® classes. It was built by a high schooler who got tired of photographing textbook pages, uploading them to ChatGPT, and asking it to make a quiz -- over and over, every unit. This is that, without the re-uploading.

The idea behind it is retrieval practice: pulling information out of your head is harder -- and sticks better -- than reading it again. Every question here is one more rep of that.

What's covered

Quizzr is built to cover every AP® subject, not just one. Six are fully live right now, with about 4,200 questions between them, every one organized by unit and topic so you can drill one piece of the course at a time instead of wading through everything at once:

  • AP® U.S. Government and Politics — 5 units, 60 topics
  • AP® Biology — 8 units, 60 topics
  • AP® U.S. History — 9 units, 105 topics
  • AP® Psychology — 5 units, 35 topics
  • AP® Chemistry — 9 units, 91 topics
  • AP® World History — 9 units, 71 topics

AP® European History, AP® Human Geography, and AP® Environmental Science are on the site with their full unit and topic outlines, but their questions are still being written -- Euro has its first couple of units done, and the other two are outline-only for now. Every course follows the College Board's own unit and topic breakdown, so the list on a subject page should match your syllabus.

How it works

Pick a subject, then a unit. From there you can quiz a single topic (10 random questions) or take a whole-unit quiz that pools questions from every topic in the unit.

Each quiz shows one multiple-choice question at a time. Questions and answer choices are shuffled every time you load a quiz, so retaking one isn't just memorizing option positions. When you pick an answer, you'll immediately see whether it was correct along with a short explanation. At the end, you'll get your score and a list of exactly which questions you missed.

What makes it different

Mastery is tracked per topic and per unit, not as one overall score -- a 78% average hides the fact that you have Unit 3 cold and Unit 5 not at all, and Quizzr shows you that split. Questions you miss on a whole-unit quiz also get weighted back in next time instead of disappearing once you've seen them, so a wrong answer turns into another rep rather than a dead end.

Two pages read that history back to you. History lists your recent quizzes and scores, and Insights turns them into a study plan: your strongest topics, the ones that need another look, and what to redo first. Both are worked out in your browser from your own scores.

There's no login and no account. Your progress is saved in your own browser, not on a server.

Heads up: Quizzr's questions are AI-generated, and AI-generated material can contain errors. Spot-check anything you plan to rely on, and treat these questions as a supplement to your textbook, class notes, and teacher -- not a replacement for them.
